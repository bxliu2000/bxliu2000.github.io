# 24

It's been a while since I've had a stream-of-consciousness type post. Mostly just wanted to organize a couple of thoughts I have on my birthday.

I guess this mostly became a perception of time post.

## The days are getting shorter.

According to random stuff I've found online, your perception of time as you turn 24/25, or around the time your brain finishes developing, starts to become much faster. This is actually the "midpoint" in terms of your perception of your entire lifetime. I can certainly see how that can be true. This last few months have passed in a blur... truly scary.

New experiences are the cure. Even greater, they can be explicitly controlled. Anecdotally, this is true. The days when I first started triathlon "felt" much longer. My perception of these long workouts have gotten shorter and easier over time. Even with a full blown training schedule the days don't feel nearly as long as they used to.

To date, I've identified three very powerful influencers on your perception of time.

The first, being change. To be pedantic, I'm referring to the magnitude of delta between your current experience and your prior experiences. This is easily demonstrated in children, where the majority of experiences are new and your perceived time pass is far slower.
Change is subjective and refers to applies to quite literally any area of your life.

The second, is pain. Conversely, pleasure.
The more pain, the slower time passes. The better time you're having, the faster time passes. Pretty simple. The slowest times of my day are during the ON portion of my intervals, The fastest are during the rest.

Is this just a special case of delta? IE can you ever get "used" to pain? or "used" to pleasure? No. Professional athletes are familiar with physical pain because they deal with it every day. However, this does not make their perception of time pass faster. Likewise, pleasure is also considered, by definition, to be a delta. However, this delta accelerates time rather than slowing it down.

Based on this, one could logically choose a macabre way of living by trying out various torture methods until they die. But is this a fulfilled life?

### But what are the implications?

Does a perceptively long life necessarily imply a life worth living?

Should you reinvent yourself every five years?

## The weight of being responsible for your own fate.

People in general underestimate the amount of agency they have in their lives. I'm generally in the camp that anything can be possible, provided you understand the conditions to succeed.

But what exactly do you want to do? This is a pretty difficult question, and honestly one which I think not thinking too hard about is what is going to yield the best results.

This relates closely to the post that "you are what you think", and also "you are who you surround yourself with".

So I turned recently turned 24. Knowing this, our overlord recommendation systems knew to post from Sam Altman somehow came across my feed.

> From a psychologist friend: "Adjusted for the subjective increase in how fast time passes, life is half over by 23 or 24. Don't waste time."

This struck a chord with me for two reasons.

The first is existential - I don't want my perceived life to be have over.

The second really exasperated this internal, nagging fear that I indeed was wasting my life. That I hadn't become nearly the man I want to be and that I'm nowhere closer to becoming that than exatly 365 days ago.

Wasting time only makes sense when considered in the context of a goal or desire. You could logically define any deviation from said goal to be a waste of time. It is easy to consider life under these lenses when you are in a hypercompetitive environment. Competition also breeds The best athletes in the world see their sport as something to infinitely optimize.

My lifestyle hasn't changed. My tolerance to risk remains the same. I've had rarely any new experiences that I feel worth. And by new experiences I mean those which emotionally resonate with me, something that makes me feel a bit deeper than an activity. More importantly, its something that money can't buy.
